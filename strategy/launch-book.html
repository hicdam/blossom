Fee and pricing evidence (what the market actually charges)
  • SGD official guidance (Rev. 2025): design fees as a percentage of build cost, sliding from about 21% at £30k to about 8 to 10% at £100k+. Stage split 25 / 40 / 10 / 25. Site supervision is an hourly "other service", which is exactly the gap Blossom productises.
  • Published designer fees: London Garden Designer, consult £195 +VAT refundable, design 10 to 15% from £3,250. Kristina Clode (Sussex), build £250 to £450 per sq m, design 10 to 15%. DP Garden Design (Alresford), 10% worked example, PM billed hourly. Nigel Philips, design days £450 to £1,500. Ann-Marie Powell, £3,000 and £5,500 design tiers.
  • Consultation conventions: premium practices charge roughly £195 to £895 and redeem it against fees; free consultations signal the volume end.
  • Homeowner pain (documented): Trading Standards prosecutions and forum threads show the repeat failure modes: defects owners cannot judge, underquoting then corner-cutting, abandonment, no one holding payment milestones.
  • Seasonality: enquiries peak in spring; the trade itself promotes autumn and winter as the planning and building season; six to twelve months from first meeting to finished garden.

Full citations in research/fees-pricing-pm-evidence.md, research/competitive-research.md and research/seo-landscape.md in the project folder.

Phase 1 → 2

The gaps, and the position they point to

Five things nobody in the three counties does:

1 · Independent client-side project management 2 · Published, packaged pricing 3 · A named year-round care programme 4 · Serious objection-handling and FAQ 5 · Premium brand + town-level SEO together

Recommended position: the accountable lead

Blossom is the practice that stands on the client's side of the whole project. We design gardens that can actually be built, and we manage the build so they get built well. Design and delivery can be bought together or separately, and the delivery half has no credible local competitor.

Why it wins: the whitespace is real (verified), the pain is documented (verified), the pricing transparency that supports it is almost unused locally (verified), and it produces revenue in every season.

Customer segments and buying situations
  • Overwhelmed dreamers. Want change, don't know where to start. Entry: consultation.
  • Design-ready, delivery-anxious. Have drawings or a designer, fear the build. Entry: independent PM. The unserved segment.
  • Time-poor professionals and families. Want one number to call. Entry: complete transformation.
  • New movers. House right, garden wrong. Entry: consultation or complete.
  • Post-renovation households. Builders gone, garden wrecked. Entry: design or complete.
  • Established garden owners. Renewal and stewardship. Entry: The Garden Year or planting design.

Conversion and measurement: forms, events, consent
  • Persistent CTA: Book a consultation in the header of every page, repeated in every page's closing band.
  • Enquiry form: name, email, phone (optional), town, need, budget band (optional), timing, message (optional), consent tick. Short enough to finish, rich enough to qualify. QR landing uses an even shorter four-field version.
  • Analytics spec (GA4, consent-gated): generate_lead on form submit with project type and budget, book_consultation_click on every CTA, phone_call_click on tel links. UTM parameters persist across the visit and land in hidden form fields, so every enquiry carries its source.
  • QR attribution: print QR points to /welcome.html?utm_source=print&utm_medium=qr&utm_campaign=local-launch-2026&utm_content=[publication]. One URL per publication, so each advert reports its own enquiries.
  • Cookie consent: UK-appropriate: analytics loads only after an explicit accept, an "essential only" choice is equally prominent, and the choice is remembered.
  • Accessibility and performance: skip links, focus states, aria labels, one h1 per page, reduced-motion respected, warm-contrast palette at AA or better, system-light static files with two font families and SVG illustration only. No frameworks, no trackers by default.

Before launch

Required inputs (nothing here was invented)

  • Founder details: name, background, photo, qualifications, memberships, insurance. Slots ready on About.
  • Contact details: phone, email, business address, legal entity name and company number (site, footer, legal pages, schema, GBP).
  • Domain: canonical URLs currently use blossomgardendesign.co.uk as a placeholder; confirm and update in one file (_build/templates.py), then rebuild.
  • Pricing sign-off: every guide figure above.
  • Form endpoint: a Formspree (or similar) URL replaces REPLACE_WITH_FORM_ENDPOINT in two forms. Until then forms demo the journey to the thank-you page.
  • GA4 ID: one line in assets/js/main.js.
  • Project photography and testimonials: as real projects complete. Optional: a Calendly link for direct diary booking.

Launch checklist

  • Site built: 17 pages, copy, SEO, schema, sitemap, robots, consent, forms, QR landing
  • Identity system: evolved mark, light/dark variants, dividers, masterplan, palette, seasonal ring
  • Automated QA passed: no em dashes, no broken internal links, no missing assets, desktop visual QA in Chrome
  • Fill required inputs above, rebuild (python3 _build/build.py), deploy to static host, connect domain, HTTPS on
  • Google Search Console: verify, submit sitemap. Google Business Profile: create with real address
  • Test the form end to end with the live endpoint; test QR codes from each printed advert; check GA4 events fire after consent
  • Legal pages: confirm entity details and have them read once by a professional

The first 90 days

Month 1: exist properly

  • Deploy site, GBP live, Houzz profile, first citations. Begin SGD or APL membership application if not already held.
  • First QR print advert in one or two local titles (Surrey and Hampshire editions), each with its own UTM content tag so the numbers settle the argument.
  • Ask every early contact for a Google review after the consultation. Publish the autumn article (done) and share it locally: Nextdoor, village magazines, local Facebook groups.

Month 2: own the questions

  • Publish the local cost guide and the "who manages a garden build" guide, the two proven content gaps.
  • Partnership outreach: landscapers who want well-specified jobs, architects mid-renovation, premium estate agents. Blossom brings them work; they bring referrals back.
  • Winter campaign message everywhere: design now, build in the quiet season, ready for summer.

Month 3: prove it

  • First case study live, even if it is a consultation-to-design story rather than a finished build. Photograph everything from day one.
  • Build the first two town pages only where a real project anchors them (research order: Alresford, Romsey, Farnham, Godalming, Cobham, Esher, Haywards Heath).
  • Review QR and GA4 data: which advert, which pages, which enquiry types. Double down where the enquiries came from, cut what stayed silent.
